Interior modelling of Steam Worlds

Exploring the interiors and thermal evolution of water-rich exoplanets

I uses structure models to connect observable properties of planets (mass, radius, temperature, age, etc.) to their bulk composition. My research focuses on steam worlds: water-rich planets whose intense stellar irradiation produces a steam atmosphere above (runaway greenhouse effect), and a deep water envelope in supercritical state (and other exotic phases of water: plasma, superionic, high pressure ices, etc.).

Thermal evolution of steam worlds

My Irradiated Ocean Planets (IOP) model explored how hot water envelopes and steam atmospheres shape planetary mass–radius relationships (Aguichine et al., 2021). In previous studies, water worlds were often thought as objects with extended oceans of condensed water: liquid or high pressure ice. This model showed that steam worlds have much larger radii, making them great candidates to explain the radii of hundreds of sub-Neptunes found with Kepler.

A planet’s radius depends on its thermal history as well as its composition. In the Steam World Evolution (SWE) model, my collaborators and I couple an interior model, which stores the planet’s energy, to a steam atmosphere model, which controls how quickly that energy escapes. This lets us follow the cooling and contraction of water-rich planets over time (Aguichine et al., 2025). Age dependence is one avenue of breaking the degeneracy between steam worlds and gas dwarfs, using revised age calculations from Gaia, and in the near future PLATO.

The latest extension, Steam World Evolution Extended in Temperature (SWEET), broadens the atmosphere grid to equilibrium temperatures of 400–1500 K, allowing us to explore a wider range of irradiated planets. The SWEET evolutionary tracks are publicly available for comparison with observations and estimates of planetary water content.
